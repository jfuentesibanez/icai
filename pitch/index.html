Step 1 · What a pitch is

A short presentation to convince an investor

The word comes from Silicon Valley: the presentation a founder gives to investors.

The elevator pitch is the shortest form: you meet the investor in the elevator, and you have until the doors open.

The goal is not the money. The goal is a second meeting.

Step 1 · Who listens

The people you pitch to are always busy

1

Investors

See hundreds of pitches every year

2

CEOs

Decide in minutes if a partner is worth it

3

Managers

Have twenty other things on the agenda today

4

A jury

Will hear ten teams in a row

So your job is simple: make it easy for them. Easy to follow, easy to remember, easy to repeat to a partner.

Step 1 · The one rule

There is no fixed structure.
Show your strengths first.

The nine blocks that follow are a checklist, not a script. Strong traction? Open with it. Strong team? Say it early.

Step 2 · The pitch deck

1

6

8

Team and roles

Block 1 of 9 · Name, logo and slogan

A name people remember, a line that says what you do

All blocks

The investor asks: “Who are you?”

✓

A name that is easy to say, spell and remember

✓

A simple logo: it must work small, in one colour

✓

A slogan: your mission in one sentence

Three names and where they come from

Airbnb comes from “AirBed & Breakfast”: the first guests slept on air mattresses. MySQL is named after My, the daughter of a co-founder. Cabify: a cab, as an app.

Watch out

A clever name you must explain. If you need a sentence to explain the name, the slogan has a problem too.

Block 2 of 9 · The problem

Describe the pain so that everybody understands it

All blocks

The investor asks: “Is this a real problem?”

✓

Say it in words your grandmother understands

✓

Show it is worth solving: how often, how bad, how many people

✓

Use what you heard in your customer interviews

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Travellers care about price. Hotels keep you away from the real city. And there is no easy way to book a room in a local person’s home.

Watch out

A problem only you have. Or a problem that is really your solution in disguise: “there is no app for X”.

Block 3 of 9 · The solution

Say what you do, clearly and in few words

All blocks

The investor asks: “So what do you do?”

✓

One sentence that answers the problem slide

✓

The benefit for the customer, not the technology

✓

No technical detail here: that comes later, if they ask

Example · Airbnb, 2008

A website where people rent out a room to travellers. Travellers save money, hosts earn money, and both share the culture of the city.

Watch out

A list of features. The investor wants to know what changes for the customer.

Block 4 of 9 · How it works

Show how it works, step by step

All blocks

The investor asks: “What does the customer actually do?”

✓

The customer’s path in three or four steps

✓

A screenshot, a prototype or a short demo is worth ten bullets

✓

Now you can add a little more detail

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Three steps: search by city, see the room and the host, book and pay online.

Watch out

A live demo with no plan B. Record a video of the demo in case the Wi-Fi fails.

Block 5 of 9 · Traction / MVP

Show what you have proven so far

All blocks

The investor asks: “Does anybody want it?”

✓

Customers or users, and how fast they grow

✓

Revenue, even if small

✓

Lessons from your interviews and experiments

✓

Other milestones: a pilot, a partner, a letter of intent

No customers yet? Then your traction is what you learned: interviews, experiments, results.

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Before they had customers, they showed demand: thousands of people already looking for a room on Couchsurfing and Craigslist.

Watch out

Vanity numbers: page visits, likes, “people said they love it”. Show what people did, not what they said.

Block 6 of 9 · Business model

Explain how you make money

All blocks

The investor asks: “Who pays, and for what?”

✓

Who pays, how much, and how often

✓

Use your Business Model Canvas as a guide, without showing all of it

✓

One simple calculation is better than a big table

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Airbnb takes a commission on each booking. The calculation on the slide: number of trips × average price × commission.

Watch out

Showing the full canvas with nine boxes full of text. Pick the two or three boxes that matter.

Block 7 of 9 · Competition

Say who your competitors are, and how you are different

All blocks

The investor asks: “Why you, and not them?”

✓

Name them: there is always competition, even if it is “doing nothing”

✓

Choose two criteria that matter to the customer

✓

Put everybody on a 2 × 2 map: you alone in one corner

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Their map: cheap or expensive, and booking online or offline. Hotels, Couchsurfing, Craigslist and others on the map; Airbnb alone in the corner “cheap and online”.

Watch out

“We have no competitors.” To an investor it means: there is no market, or you have not looked.

Block 8 of 9 · Size of the opportunity

Show how big the market is

All blocks

The investor asks: “How big can this get?”

✓

Total market: everybody with the problem

✓

The part you can serve with your product

✓

The part you can realistically win in a few years

TAM, SAM, SOM →

Example · Airbnb, 2008

Two billion trips per year in the world. 560 million of them budget and booked online. Their target: 15% of that, 84 million trips.

Watch out

Starting with “the market is 500 billion euros, if we get 1%…”. Build it from the bottom: customers × price.

Block 9 of 9 · Team and roles

Show why you are the right people

All blocks

The investor asks: “Why should I trust you with this?”

✓

Who you are, with a photo

✓

What background makes you right for this problem

✓

Who takes care of what in the team

Example · Airbnb, 2008

On the slide: the founders, with their roles and one line each about what they had built before.

Watch out

Titles with no content (“CEO, CTO, CMO” in a team of three students). Say what each person actually does.

Step 3 · Size of the opportunity

Three circles, from everybody to your customers

TAM

Total Available Market

Everybody who has the problem, in the whole world.

Example: All LED lighting

SAM

Serviceable Available Market

The part you can reach with your product and your channels.

Example: Colour-changing LED for the home

SOM

Serviceable Obtainable Market

The part you can realistically win, with your competition, countries and sales team.

Example: Your sales goal for year 3

Step 4 · How to give it

Six rules for the day of the pitch

1

Start with a question

The first ten seconds decide if they listen. “How did you book your last trip?”

2

Clear, brief, concise

One idea per slide. If a slide needs a paragraph, it is two slides.

3

Plan the turns

If you speak in turns, decide who says what, and how you pass the word.

4

Do not memorise

Learn the order and the key sentences. A memorised text sounds like a recording.

5

Practise the time

Rehearse with a timer, out loud, at least three times. You will be slower than you think.

6

Be convincing

Believe in it, look at the audience, and finish with what you need from them.

Step 4 · Remember

You do not need to tell everything.
You need a second meeting.

Your turn · This week

Prepare your team’s pitch

1

Choose your blocks

Go through the nine blocks. Put your strongest one early. Drop what you do not have yet.

2

One slide per block

One idea per slide, big text, one picture or one number.

3

Split the voices

Everybody in the team speaks. Decide who says what.

4

Rehearse with a timer

Out loud, standing, at least three times. Cut until it fits.

Use everything from the last sessions: your hypotheses, your experiments and your interviews are your traction.

Make it easy for them.

Then ask for the second meeting.
