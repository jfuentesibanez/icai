Step 1 · Where we are

You have 3 critical hypotheses. Now we test them.

D · Desirable

I think 6 in 10 students away from home spend more than 40 euros a week on delivery

F · Feasible

I think 2 kitchens in Madrid will cook 200 meals a day for us

V · Viable

I think we can cook and deliver a meal for less than 4 euros and sell it for 6

Each one ended with “and we will know it is true when…”. Today we build that “when”.

Step 1 · What changed

Our old rule: your first MVP with less than 100 euros, in less than a day

Low fidelity

Paper, cardboard, storyboard

A first draft. No interaction at all.

Medium fidelity

Clickable mockup, landing page

Incomplete. Some parts done by hand behind the screen.

High fidelity

Real software

Close to the final product. Needed a developer and weeks of work.

Low fidelity was the default because building real things was slow and expensive.

Step 1 · What changed

Now one person with an AI tool can build a working product

BeforeNow

How longWeeksHours or days

The hard partBuilding itKnowing what to build, and for whom

So is it still a prototype? Often it is a real product. The question changes: not “can we build it?” but “should we?”

Step 1 · What changed

AI made one risk cheaper. Not the other two.

D

Desirable

Do customers want it? Still yours to prove. AI cannot want things for your customers.

F

Feasible

Can we build it? For software, much cheaper now. Hardware, kitchens and partners: not yet.

V

Viable

Do we earn more than we spend? Still yours to prove. Your rivals can build fast too.

The trap: build the wrong thing, faster. A working app feels like evidence. It is not.

Step 2 · Choosing a level

Real is cheap now. Why not always start there?

1

A real app answers “can we build it?”. That is rarely your riskiest question.

2

With no users, a real app teaches nothing. Getting users is the experiment.

3

After weeks on a product, you defend it. You stop listening.

Start at the cheapest level that could prove your hypothesis wrong.

Step 3 · Experiments · The test card

We believe that [hypothesis].
To verify that, we will [experiment].
And measure [metric].
We are right if [pass mark].

Source: Strategyzer Test Card · Bland and Osterwalder, Testing Business Ideas, Wiley, 2019

Step 3 · Case: home-cooked meals for students

One test card, filled in

We believe that

students away from home in Madrid will pay 6 euros for a home-cooked meal.

To verify that, we will

build a landing page with a 6-euro pre-order button and show it with Instagram ads to students in Madrid for 7 days.

And measure

how many visitors pay.

We are right if

at least 20 of the first 100 visitors pre-order.

Level:

4 · Real (they pay)

Time: 1 week

Evidence: strong

Step 3 · Experiments

What AI adds to an experiment

1

Three versions, not one

Three landing pages with three promises, in the time one used to take. Let the data choose.

2

Real usage data

A working product shows who comes back after a week. That is stronger than any “I would use it”.

3

A faster loop

Build on Monday, show it on Tuesday, decide on Friday. One learning cycle per week.

But: an AI’s opinion of your idea is not evidence. Only real customers can prove a hypothesis wrong.

Step 3 · Experiments

Play fair with the people in your test

1

After a fake door or a Wizard of Oz, tell people the truth.

2

Never keep money for something you will not deliver. Refund it.

3

Ask before you collect personal data, and collect only what you need.

4

Tell people when they are talking to an AI, not to a person.

A test that tricks people damages your brand before it exists.
