Step 1 · Where we are

You have 3 critical hypotheses. Now we test them.

D · Desirable

I think 6 in 10 students away from home spend more than 40 euros a week on delivery

F · Feasible

I think 2 kitchens in Madrid will cook 200 meals a day for us

V · Viable

I think we can cook and deliver a meal for less than 4 euros and sell it for 6

Each one ended with “and we will know it is true when…”. Today we build that “when”.

Step 1 · What changed

Our old rule: your first MVP with less than 100 euros, in less than a day

Low fidelity

Paper, cardboard, storyboard

A first draft. No interaction at all.

Medium fidelity

Clickable mockup, landing page

Incomplete. Some parts done by hand behind the screen.

High fidelity

Real software

Close to the final product. Needed a developer and weeks of work.

Low fidelity was the default because building real things was slow and expensive.

Step 1 · What changed

Now one person with an AI tool can build a working product

BeforeNow
Who buildsA developerYou, describing it in plain words
How longWeeksHours or days
What you getScreens that look realLogin, data, payments, AI features
The hard partBuilding itKnowing what to build, and for whom

So is it still a prototype? Often it is a real product. The question changes: not “can we build it?” but “should we?”

Step 1 · What changed

Building is no longer the hard part.
Knowing what to build is.

Step 1 · What changed

AI made one risk cheaper. Not the other two.

D

Desirable

Do customers want it? Still yours to prove. AI cannot want things for your customers.

F

Feasible

Can we build it? For software, much cheaper now. Hardware, kitchens and partners: not yet.

V

Viable

Do we earn more than we spend? Still yours to prove. Your rivals can build fast too.

The trap: build the wrong thing, faster. A working app feels like evidence. It is not.

Step 2 · Choosing a level

Real is cheap now. Why not always start there?

1

A real app answers “can we build it?”. That is rarely your riskiest question.

2

With no users, a real app teaches nothing. Getting users is the experiment.

3

After weeks on a product, you defend it. You stop listening.

Start at the cheapest level that could prove your hypothesis wrong.

Step 3 · Experiments · The test card

We believe that [hypothesis].
To verify that, we will [experiment].
And measure [metric].
We are right if [pass mark].

Source: Strategyzer Test Card · Bland and Osterwalder, Testing Business Ideas, Wiley, 2019

Step 3 · Case: home-cooked meals for students

One test card, filled in

We believe that

students away from home in Madrid will pay 6 euros for a home-cooked meal.

To verify that, we will

build a landing page with a 6-euro pre-order button and show it with Instagram ads to students in Madrid for 7 days.

And measure

how many visitors pay.

We are right if

at least 20 of the first 100 visitors pre-order.

Level:

4 · Real (they pay)

Time: 1 week

Evidence: strong

Step 3 · Experiments

What AI adds to an experiment

1

Three versions, not one

Three landing pages with three promises, in the time one used to take. Let the data choose.

2

Real usage data

A working product shows who comes back after a week. That is stronger than any “I would use it”.

3

A faster loop

Build on Monday, show it on Tuesday, decide on Friday. One learning cycle per week.

But: an AI’s opinion of your idea is not evidence. Only real customers can prove a hypothesis wrong.

Step 3 · Experiments

Play fair with the people in your test

1

After a fake door or a Wizard of Oz, tell people the truth.

2

Never keep money for something you will not deliver. Refund it.

3

Ask before you collect personal data, and collect only what you need.

4

Tell people when they are talking to an AI, not to a person.

A test that tricks people damages your brand before it exists.

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Problem interviews

D

← Level 1

All cards

A conversation of about 30 minutes about the customer’s problem and how they solve it today. You do not show or sell your idea.

How to run it

1

Find 10 people from your segment. Not friends, not family.

2

Ask about the last time it happened: what, when, how much it cost.

3

Listen for what they already do, and pay, to solve it.

4

Write your notes the same day.

What you measure

How many describe the problem without your help, and what they do today to solve it.

Example

Case: “Tell me about the last time you had dinner at home. Who cooked?” Not: “Would you use our app?”

With AI

An AI note-taker writes the transcript, and AI can group quotes by theme. Read the quotes yourself before you trust the groups.

Watch out

Compliments and “I would buy it” are not data. Ask about the past, not the future.

Tools: Google Meet, Calendly, an AI note-taker · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak: what they say · Source: Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test, 2013

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Expert interviews

DFV

← Level 1

All cards

Conversations with people who know your market from the inside: suppliers, sellers, regulators, former founders.

How to run it

1

List who sells to, supplies or regulates your customer.

2

Ask for 20 minutes through LinkedIn or the alumni network.

3

Ask about numbers: prices, margins, volumes, failures.

4

End with: “Who else should I talk to?”

What you measure

Facts that confirm or break your F and V hypotheses: costs, rules, channels.

Example

Case: the manager of a student residence explains who decides on food services, and how often that contract changes.

With AI

AI can prepare your questions and summarise public reports before the call.

Watch out

Experts know the past of the market, not always its future. One expert is one opinion.

Tools: LinkedIn, the Comillas alumni network · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak to medium

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Shadowing

DF

← Level 1

All cards

You spend time with the customer while they do the task today, in their real place.

How to run it

1

Ask permission to watch for one or two hours.

2

Do not help, and do not explain your idea.

3

Write down every step, tool, delay and workaround.

4

Ask “why?” only at the end.

What you measure

The steps, the time they take and the workarounds you see.

Example

Case: you watch a student plan meals for a week: the shopping list, the supermarket, the food thrown away on Friday.

With AI

Give your notes and photos to an AI to draw the process map. Check it against what you saw.

Watch out

People change what they do when someone watches. Repeat with several people.

Tools: A notebook, a phone camera (with permission) · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Medium: what they do

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Communities

D

← Level 1

All cards

You read what customers already write in public about the problem: forums, groups, and reviews of competitors.

How to run it

1

Find 3 to 5 places where your customer talks.

2

Search for the problem in their words, not yours.

3

Copy real quotes and count how often each pain appears.

4

Read the 1-star and 2-star reviews of competitors.

What you measure

How often the problem appears, and the words people use for it.

Example

Case: search student forums for “cocinar en piso de estudiantes”, and read the reviews of delivery apps.

With AI

AI can read hundreds of posts and group them by pain. Ask it for the exact quotes, and check them.

Watch out

Online, angry people write more than happy people. It shows the problem exists, not how big it is.

Tools: Reddit, Forocoches, Google Maps and app store reviews · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Trends and data

DV

← Level 1

All cards

Public data to see if the problem and the market are growing, and how big they are.

How to run it

1

Choose 3 or 4 search terms your customer would use.

2

Compare them in Google Trends over 5 years and by region.

3

Look for official statistics and industry reports.

4

Write each number next to its source.

What you measure

The direction of the trend, and a first estimate of market size, with sources.

Example

Case: searches for “comida casera a domicilio” in Madrid, and the number of students who live away from home, from official statistics.

With AI

AI deep research finds and summarises reports fast. Open every source: AI sometimes invents numbers.

Watch out

A trend is not demand for your product. Check that each number comes from the study it claims.

Tools: Google Trends, INE, Eurostat, AI deep research · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 1 · Say

Synthetic users

Not evidence

← Level 1

All cards

AI personas that answer as if they were your customer. Useful to practise, never to decide.

How to run it

1

Describe your segment to the AI in detail.

2

Run your interview script with it.

3

Find the confusing or leading questions, and fix them.

4

Then interview real people.

What you measure

Nothing about the market. Only the quality of your interview script.

Example

Case: you rehearse with “a 20-year-old student from Valencia who lives in Madrid” and find two questions that push the answer.

With AI

This technique is all AI. That is the reason for the warning.

Watch out

The AI tells you what sounds likely, not what is true for your customers. Its answers are not evidence.

Tools: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini · Effort: Low · Evidence: None

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

Storyboard

D

← Level 2

All cards

A comic strip of 6 to 8 frames: the customer’s problem, the moment they find you, and their life after.

How to run it

1

Write the story in 6 to 8 steps.

2

Draw the frames, or generate them with AI.

3

Show it and ask the customer to tell the story back to you.

4

Ask which frame is not true for them.

What you measure

Which frames they recognise from their own life, and where they say “that is not me”.

Example

Case: Monday of exam week, an empty fridge, no time to shop… and a home-cooked meal at the door at 9 pm.

With AI

AI image tools draw the frames in minutes, with the same character in each one.

Watch out

A nice drawing makes people kind. Ask about their life, not about the drawing.

Tools: ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney, Storyboard That · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

Explainer video

D

← Level 2

All cards

A video of about one minute that shows the problem and your solution, and ends with one action: sign up or pre-order.

How to run it

1

Write the script: problem, solution, how it works, the action.

2

Produce it with AI video, or record your screen.

3

Put it on a landing page or in an ad.

4

Count who takes the action after watching.

What you measure

The % of viewers who take the action at the end.

Example

Classic: Dropbox showed a demo video before the product was ready, to see if people wanted it (Ries, The Lean Startup, 2011).

With AI

AI video and AI voices make a credible video in an afternoon, without a camera.

Watch out

Views are not interest. Only the action at the end counts.

Tools: Veo, Sora, Runway, ElevenLabs, Loom · Effort: Low · Evidence: Medium: what they do

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

Product box

DV

← Level 2

All cards

You design the box, the label or the sales sheet of a product that does not exist yet, and show it as if it were for sale.

How to run it

1

Choose the 3 main benefits and a price.

2

Design the box or the sheet.

3

Put it next to real products, on a table or in a shop.

4

Ask them to choose one, and to react to the price.

What you measure

Which benefit they read first, if they choose yours, and what they say about the price.

Example

Case: a box “Exam week pack · 5 dinners · 35 euros” next to a flyer of a delivery app.

With AI

AI renders show a realistic package before you print anything.

Watch out

Liking the box is not buying it. Add a way to pay or to sign up.

Tools: Canva, AI image tools, Packhelp for real boxes · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

Clickable mockup

D

← Level 2

All cards

Screens that link to each other like a real app. Nothing works behind them.

How to run it

1

Draw the 5 to 8 key screens.

2

Link them in Figma.

3

Give a user one task, for example “order dinner for Thursday”.

4

Watch in silence. Note where they get lost.

What you measure

How many finish the task, and the screen where the others stop.

Example

Case: 5 students try to order a meal for Thursday. Do they find where to choose the day?

With AI

Figma Make and similar tools turn a text description into screens.

Watch out

It tests if they understand it, not if they want it.

Tools: Figma, Figma Make, Balsamiq · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

Paper and 3D print

DF

← Level 2

All cards

A physical model of your product, in cardboard or printed in 3D, that people can hold and use.

How to run it

1

Build the simplest model with the right shape and size.

2

Give it to users with a real task.

3

Watch hands and faces, not only words.

4

Change it and test again.

What you measure

Problems of size, weight and use, and what people try to do with it.

Example

Case: a 3D-printed lunch box that keeps two dishes apart. Does it fit in a backpack?

With AI

AI tools can turn a text or a photo into a 3D model ready to print.

Watch out

A model says nothing about cost, materials or manufacturing. Those are other hypotheses.

Tools: Cardboard, tape, a 3D printer · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 2 · Show

5-second test

D

← Level 2

All cards

You show a page or an image for 5 seconds, then ask what they remember.

How to run it

1

Choose the page: home, landing page or ad.

2

Show it for 5 seconds.

3

Ask: what is it, who is it for, what can you do there?

4

Compare the answers with your value proposition.

What you measure

The % of people who can say what you offer, and to whom.

Example

Case: after 5 seconds, can a student say “home-cooked meals delivered to students”?

With AI

Generate 3 versions of the page and test them all on the same day.

Watch out

It tests clarity, not desire.

Tools: Lyssna, Maze · Effort: Low · Evidence: Weak

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Landing page

D

← Level 3

All cards

A one-page website with your value proposition and one button: sign up, join the list, or pre-order.

How to run it

1

Write the headline: for whom, what problem, what result.

2

Add one button with one action.

3

Bring visitors: ads, communities, your network.

4

Count visits and clicks on the button.

What you measure

Conversion: people who click or leave an email, divided by visitors.

Example

Case: “Home-cooked dinners for students in Madrid, 6 euros” and one button: “Join the list”.

With AI

Lovable or Framer build the page from a description in minutes. Make three versions with three promises.

Watch out

With no visitors, a landing page teaches nothing. Note where each visitor came from.

Tools: Framer, Carrd, Lovable, Tally for forms · Effort: Low · Evidence: Medium: what they do

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Ad test

D

← Level 3

All cards

Small paid ads to the same audience, each with a different promise. You see which one people click.

How to run it

1

Define the audience: age, place, interests.

2

Write 3 ads, each with a different promise.

3

Give each one the same small budget and the same days.

4

Compare the click rate and the cost per sign-up.

What you measure

Click rate and cost per sign-up, for each promise.

Example

Case: “Eat like at home” vs “Spend less on delivery” vs “More time to study”.

With AI

AI writes the text and makes the images for many versions in one hour.

Watch out

Clicks are cheap. Send them to a landing page and measure the next step.

Tools: Meta Ads, Google Ads, TikTok Ads · Effort: Low (a small budget) · Evidence: Medium: what they do

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Fake door

D

← Level 3

All cards

A button or menu option for something that does not exist yet. You count the clicks, then you explain.

How to run it

1

Add the button where the real feature would be.

2

On click, show: “Coming soon. Shall we tell you when it is ready?”

3

Count clicks and emails.

4

Remove it when you have the answer.

What you measure

The % of users who click it, compared with the other options on the page.

Example

Case: on the landing page, “Meal plan for the whole month” next to “Order one dinner”.

With AI

On an AI-built site, adding a new fake door takes minutes.

Watch out

Tell the truth right after the click. Do not leave a fake door open for months.

Tools: Any web tool, plus PostHog or Google Analytics · Effort: Low · Evidence: Medium: what they do

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Wizard of Oz

DF

← Level 3

All cards

The customer uses something that looks automatic. Behind the screen, a person does the work by hand.

How to run it

1

Build only the front: a form, a chat, one app screen.

2

When an order arrives, do the work by hand.

3

Deliver the result as if it were automatic.

4

Write down each step you did: that is your future product.

What you measure

Repeat use and satisfaction, and how much work each order needs.

Example

Classic: early Zappos showed photos of shoes from local shops, and bought the pair only when someone ordered it (Ries, The Lean Startup, 2011).

With AI

Today the “wizard” can be an AI model, with you checking each answer before it goes out.

Watch out

Do not promise a speed you cannot meet by hand. Tell users the truth when the test ends.

Tools: A form or a chat, Google Sheets, WhatsApp, an AI model · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Strong: they use it

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Concierge

DF

← Level 3

All cards

You deliver the service by hand to a few customers, and they know it is you.

How to run it

1

Find 5 to 10 customers.

2

Deliver the full service yourself.

3

Talk with them at every delivery.

4

Write down what you do each time, and how long it takes.

What you measure

Do they come back? Would they pay? Which steps could be automated?

Example

Case: for two weeks you cook for 10 students and deliver the dinners by bike.

With AI

AI helps you plan, write messages and keep notes, so you can serve more people by hand.

Watch out

It does not scale, and it is not meant to. Do not confuse it with your business model.

Tools: WhatsApp, Google Sheets, an AI assistant · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Strong: they use it

Experiment card · Level 3 · Fake

Frankenstein

F

← Level 3

All cards

You join existing products to deliver your service before you build your own. It can even use a competitor’s product (“imposter judo”).

How to run it

1

Draw your service as a chain of steps.

2

For each step, find a product that already does it.

3

Connect them with no-code tools.

4

Run it with real customers.

What you measure

Does the service work from start to end? Where does it break?

Example

Case: orders by form, a shared spreadsheet, a partner kitchen and a local courier service.

With AI

Automation tools with AI connect the pieces and write the messages for you.

Watch out

You depend on other companies’ prices and rules. Test the costs (V) separately.

Tools: Zapier, Make, n8n, Airtable, Tally · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Medium

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Working app

DFV

← Level 4

All cards

A real product with login, data, payments and AI features. You describe it in plain words, and an AI tool builds it.

How to run it

1

Write one page: the user, the problem, the 3 core functions.

2

Build the smallest version with an AI tool.

3

Put it in front of real users within days.

4

Measure what they do, not what they say.

What you measure

First use, return after one week, and payment.

Example

Case: a web app where students choose their dinners for the week and pay.

With AI

This is the new part: one person can build it in days. The risk moves to “will anyone use it?”

Watch out

Real users mean real personal data (GDPR) and real security. And do not fall in love with it.

Tools: Lovable, Bolt, v0, Replit, Claude Code, Cursor · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Strong: they use it

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Pre-sale

V

← Level 4

All cards

You sell before the product is ready: a real price and a real payment, with a refund if you do not deliver.

How to run it

1

Set a price and a delivery date.

2

Add a payment button, for example a payment link.

3

Bring visitors, as with a landing page.

4

Deliver, or refund everybody.

What you measure

The % of visitors who pay, at the price you set.

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Letter of intent

V

← Level 4

All cards

B2B: a document in which a customer says they intend to buy if you deliver. It is not a contract, but it is a commitment.

How to run it

1

Do the interviews and the demo first.

2

Write one page: what, when, approximate price, conditions.

3

Ask the person who decides the purchase to sign it.

4

Count signatures. Note who said no, and why.

What you measure

The number of signed letters, and the size of each possible order.

Example

Case: two student residences sign that they will run a pilot in January if the price per dinner is below an agreed amount.

With AI

AI drafts the letter and adapts it to each customer. A lawyer checks the final version.

Watch out

Non-binding means they can change their mind. A paid pilot is stronger.

Tools: A one-page document, an e-signature tool · Effort: Low · Evidence: Strong: they commit

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Pilot

FV

← Level 4

All cards

B2B: one real customer uses your product for a fixed time and budget, with a success metric agreed in advance.

How to run it

1

Agree on the scope, the time and the price.

2

Write the success metric into the contract.

3

Deliver, and meet the customer every week.

4

At the end: continue, grow or stop.

What you measure

The agreed metric, and whether they want to continue at full price.

Example

Case: one residence offers your dinners to its students for 4 weeks.

With AI

AI lets you adapt the product to this customer quickly, during the pilot.

Watch out

A free pilot proves little. Ask for a payment, even a small one.

Tools: A short contract · Effort: High · Evidence: Strongest: they pay

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Crowdfunding

DV

← Level 4

All cards

You present the product to the public and ask them to pay now. You only build it if you reach the goal.

How to run it

1

Make the video and the page, with rewards and prices.

2

Build an audience before launch: a waiting list.

3

Launch with a fixed goal and a deadline.

4

Deliver what you promised, on time.

What you measure

Number of backers, money raised, and average payment per backer.

Example

Classic: Pebble funded its first smartwatch on Kickstarter in 2012, before mass production.

With AI

AI helps with the video, the page and the messages to backers.

Watch out

Do not expect the platform to bring the audience: bring your own. A failure, or a late delivery, is public.

Tools: Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Verkami · Effort: High · Evidence: Strong: they pay

Experiment card · Level 4 · Real

Usage data and A/B

DV

← Level 4

All cards

You measure what real users do in the product, and you show two versions to two random groups to see which works better.

How to run it

1

Choose one metric that matters: sign-up, first order, return.

2

Make version A and version B, with only one difference.

3

Split users at random between A and B.

4

Wait for enough data. Then decide.

What you measure

The difference in your metric between A and B, and return rate week by week.

Example

Case: A shows “6 euros per dinner”, B shows “30 euros per week”. Which one sells more?

With AI

AI makes new versions fast, so you can test more ideas. You still need enough users for each one.

Watch out

With few users, a difference can be pure chance. Do not stop the test on the first day.

Tools: PostHog, Google Analytics, Optimizely · Effort: Medium · Evidence: Strong: they use it
