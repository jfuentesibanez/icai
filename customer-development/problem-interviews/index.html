Step 1 · Why a problem interview

Two kinds of interview. Today, only the first one.

First · Today

Problem interview

Does this customer have the problem? How often? How do they solve it today, and what does it cost them? You never show your idea.

Later

Solution interview

You show a first version of your solution. Does it solve the problem they told you about? Would they pay for it?

Do not mix them. When you show your idea too early, people start to talk about your idea, not about their life.

Step 1 · The one rule

Talk about their life,
not about your idea.

Step 1 · The Mom Test

Even your mother will lie to you. She loves you.

You: Mom, I have a business idea. Do you have five minutes?

Mom: Of course, dear.

What she means: I am proud of you and I do not want to hurt you.

You: You use your iPad a lot, right?

Mom: Yes, it is great.

What she means: I use it to read email on the sofa. Nothing else.

You: Would you buy a cookbook app?

Mom: Sounds nice! Does it have vegan recipes?

What she means: I have plenty of cookbooks, and I have never bought an app.

Source: Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test, 2013

Step 1 · The Mom Test

The same conversation, done right

When did you last use your iPad? For what?

Have you ever used it in the kitchen?

Have you ever bought an app? Which one? How much did it cost?

When did you last buy a cookbook? Why?

Do you use your cookbooks? What do you dislike about them?

No idea, no app, no “would you”. Only her real life, in the past. She cannot lie to be nice, because there is nothing to be nice about.

Step 2 · Bad interviews

Three kinds of answer that look like data, and are not

Compliments

People are kind. A compliment costs them nothing and tells you nothing.

“What a great idea!”

Ideas

People love to give opinions, also about business. Do not write down the idea: ask why they want it.

“You should add a chat!”

Wishful thinking

The future and the conditional. Everybody is a perfect customer in the future.

“I would buy it.” “I will use it.”

Your job: bring the conversation back to facts from the past.

Step 2 · Good interviews

Eight rules for interviewing customers

1

Beginner’s mind

Listen and do not interpret. Look for the unexpected.

2

Listen more than you talk

You are there to learn, not to convince. They talk 80%.

3

Facts, not opinions

Not “Would you…?” but “When was the last time…?”

4

Ask why

“Why is that important to you?” Ask it until you reach the real reason.

5

Learn, do not sell

Not “Would you buy it?” but “How did you choose the last time?”

6

No solution too early

Do not explain your idea. Ask what bothers them most.

7

Follow up

Ask permission to contact them again, to test a prototype later.

8

Leave the door open

Always end with: “Who else should I talk to?”

Source: Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda and Smith, Value Proposition Design, Wiley, 2014

Step 2 · Case: home-cooked meals for students

Change the question, not the topic

Do not askAsk instead
Would you buy a meal plan for students?How much did you spend on food last week?
Do you like healthy food?What did you eat yesterday? Where and when?
Would you use an app that plans your meals?How do you decide what to eat on a weekday?
Is eating well a problem for you?What are the three hardest things about eating well during exams?

Open questions, about the past. A yes or a no teaches you nothing.

Step 2 · Ask why

The first answer is rarely the real one

“I order delivery three times a week.”

→ You: Why?

“I get home from the lab at nine.”

→ You: Why does that matter?

“At nine I am too tired to cook.”

→ You: Why not cook earlier, or at the weekend?

“I never have food at home. I do not plan my shopping.”

The problem is not cooking. It is planning and shopping. A different business.

Step 2 · Good interviews

The practical side

1

One person per interview, 20 to 60 minutes. Watch the face, not only the words.

2

Go in pairs: one person asks, the other one writes.

3

Take notes on paper, in front of them. Do not record: people talk less.

4

Ask an important question twice, in other words, to check the answer.

Be ready to hear things you do not like. That is the point. And always say thank you.
